Overall impression

The strategic work here is good. The first sentence names five buyer segments and two technical concerns — low-latency execution and risk controls — which is more specificity than most agency pages in this category manage anywhere on the page. The visual system is coherent: one card treatment, one CTA vocabulary, one accent.

The problem is that the page is built like a brochure and priced like a sales asset. Its single conversion surface — the contact form — cannot be completed with a keyboard, because the required consent checkbox is display: none and therefore absent from the tab order. Its primary button ships with a .disabled class and cursor: not-allowed, so even a mouse user reads it as broken before they start typing. And every green CTA on the page sits at 2.53:1 against its own label.

The biggest opportunity is not a redesign. It is a week of correctness work on the conversion path plus one darker green, after which the existing layout would perform noticeably better than it does now.

First impression — the two-second read

What draws the eye first: the phone renders on the right, not the headline. They occupy roughly 45% of the hero, carry the highest local contrast on the page (black candlesticks and red percentages on white UI), and sit at the natural focal point. The headline — white at 2.7:1 over a mid-green gradient — is the second thing you see.

That ordering is defensible for a product company. For a development services firm it is backwards: the mockups are someone else's product, and a visitor who reads them first spends their attention on a UI they cannot buy.

Emotional read: competent, corporate, slightly generic. The green-and-white palette is the default for fintech services and does nothing to distinguish itexus from the twenty other firms this buyer is shortlisting.

Is the purpose clear? Yes, immediately, and that is a real win. The H1 is literal, the subhead qualifies the audience, and the single CTA is unambiguous.

Usability

Ordered by impact on the page's ability to generate a lead.

Critical

Keyboard users cannot submit the contact form

The required consent checkbox input[name="policy"] is hidden with display: none and replaced by a styled label. display: none removes an element from both the tab order and the accessibility tree, so there is no way to satisfy the requirement without a mouse.

Tab order inside the form: text · text · email · text · textarea · a · button
Consent input: display: none — not present

FixKeep the real input in the DOM and clip it visually (position:absolute; opacity:0; width:1px; height:1px) rather than removing it, and style the visible box from :checked / :focus-visible on the sibling. Add a visible focus ring on the proxy box.

Critical

The submit button looks broken before you touch it

“Get in touch” renders grey (#606060) with a 40%-opacity white label and cursor: not-allowed from first paint. The class list is form-submit disabled submit — the disabled state is the default state, released only once consent is ticked. Every visitor's first impression of the form is a dead button, and per the finding above, a keyboard user can never revive it.

Label contrast: rgba(255,255,255,.4) over #606060 → 2.40:1

FixShip the button enabled and validate on submit, surfacing a specific inline message next to the unchecked consent box. A disabled control that never explains itself is a dead end; an enabled control that explains the blocker is an instruction. If you keep the disabled state, raise the label to at least 4.5:1 and pair it with visible helper text.

Critical

All ten FAQ answers are mouse-only

Each row is a <div class="itx-faq-new__question"> wrapping an <h3>. There is no <button>, no role, no tabindex, and no aria-expanded anywhere in the block — only cursor: pointer. Keyboard and screen-reader users cannot open any answer, including the two that matter most commercially: cost and timeline.

.itx-faq-new [aria-expanded] → 0 matches
tabindex: null · role: null · cursor: pointer

FixWrap each question in a <button aria-expanded> controlling the answer panel by id, or replace the whole block with native <details>/<summary>, which gives you keyboard support, state announcement, and in-page find for free.

Critical

A logo collides with the flagship case study's title

The Coinstar mark is absolutely positioned and lands on top of the heading “Cryptocurrency e-Wallet Application Ecosystem…” at 1440px, rendering both illegible. At 390px it moves onto the thumbnail image instead. This is the first item in the proof section — the moment the page asks to be believed.

FixPut the client mark in its own grid row above the title rather than positioning it absolutely, and cap it at a fixed height so logos of differing aspect ratios sit on one baseline.

Moderate

The form has no labels and no required-field marking

All five visible fields are placeholder-only. The placeholder disappears the moment a user types, so anyone who pauses mid-form, tabs back, or uses autofill loses the field's identity — and screen readers get nothing. Three of the five are required (name, email, message) with no visual or programmatic indication.

5 visible fields · labels: 0 · aria-label: 0 · required: 3 (unmarked)

FixAdd persistent labels above each field — a float-label pattern preserves the current density if the layout matters. Mark the three required fields and drop “Company name” and “Phone number” to optional-labelled, which also signals a lower commitment.

Moderate

The proof section proves the wrong thing

The featured case study is a crypto e-wallet, not a trading platform. The three studies that are trading projects are all badged “Under NDA” with no client, no metric, and no outcome — “An automated, real-time trading system that allows administrators to configure trading strategies” is a feature description, not evidence. Of five testimonials, only one comes from a trading or investing firm, and it is the only anonymous one.

FixNDA does not prevent quantified outcomes. Replace the descriptions with anonymised numbers a buyer in this category actually weighs — order round-trip latency, peak messages per second, instruments supported, months to first live trade, exchange or broker integrations shipped. Promote the “Risk Management Director, Investing Fund” quote to the top of the carousel; it is the most relevant one you have.

Moderate

Forty-six capability bullets, no commercial anchor

Four separate sections list what itexus can build — 21 solution items, 6 must-haves, 6 service areas, 13 core features — with almost no overlap in structure and heavy overlap in content (“Trading Execution Middleware” appears in two of them). Nowhere does the page state a price band, an engagement model, a team shape, or a reason to choose this firm over a competitor. The FAQ answers the cost question with “there is no fixed cost” and a link away from the page.

FixCollapse the four lists into two — “what we build” and “how we build it” — and reclaim the space for the two questions the buyer actually has: what does an engagement cost, and what have you shipped in production. A starting band (“discovery from $X, MVP typically $Y–Z”) qualifies leads rather than losing them.

Moderate

Chat widget overlaps live controls

At 1440px the chat bubble sits on top of the cookie banner's “Accept All” button. At 390px it covers the “Trading Software Solutions We Develop” heading and, further down, body copy in the case study card. The cookie banner itself offers “Accept All” and “Cookie Settings” with no reject control of equal prominence.

FixSuppress the chat launcher while the consent bar is visible, and give it a lower z-index than page headings. Add a “Reject non-essential” button beside “Accept All”.

Minor

A 470px decorative band interrupts the argument

Between the must-haves list and the case studies sits a full-width photograph of two phones carrying no caption, no data, and no relationship to the sections either side of it. On mobile it costs a full screen of scroll at the exact point where the reader is deciding whether to continue.

FixCut it, or earn it — the same slot could hold an architecture diagram of the execution path, which is the one visual that would actually differentiate this page from a generic services page.

Minor

Award badges render below legibility

The eleven trust badges under the testimonials display between 40px and 69px wide. None is readable, the first is clipped at the container edge, and each is served at three to eight times its display size.

FixKeep three or four — Clutch 4.9/40 reviews is the only one carrying real signal — at a legible size, and drop the rest.

The H1 has no dominance

The page headline and every section heading are set at the same 40px / 600. The only thing separating the page's thesis from “FAQs” is position. In a 1440px canvas with a 640px-tall hero, the H1 is confined to a 625px column at 40px — small enough that the phone renders win the hero by default.

Raise the H1 to roughly 56–64px and pull section H2s down to 30–32px. That single change fixes both the hero focal order and the flatness of the scroll, at no layout cost.

Line length is more than double the readable maximum

In the “Must-haves for Automated Trading App Development” block, 14px body copy runs inside a 1196px box — approximately 174 characters per line against a comfortable range of 45–75. The eye loses its place returning to the left margin, so the section reads as a wall and gets skipped. The right half of the card is empty while this happens.

Cap prose at max-width: 68ch and run the six must-haves as a two-column grid. The section shortens, the measure halves, and the dead space disappears in one move.

The bento grid buries its own headline category

“Trading Platforms” — the category that names the page and contains custom platforms, mobile apps, OTC and HFT systems — sits mid-left in the masonry, below the fold of the section, while “Data & Analytics” and “Execution & Middleware” take the top row. Masonry ordering is being decided by card height rather than by importance. Pin “Trading Platforms” to the first cell and let the rest flow.

On the green. #25BB4D is the brand's most-used colour and it fails on every button that carries a word. Two routes out. Darken it for text-bearing surfaces — #0F7A35 gives 5.45:1 with white and still reads as the same brand green; keep #25BB4D for icons, borders, rules and hover states where no text sits on it. Or keep the bright green and switch the labels to near-black: #0B1F12 on #25BB4D measures 8.2:1. The first route preserves the current look more faithfully.

On the hero. The background under the headline sits around #82A4A2. A left-to-right scrim bringing it to roughly #3A5B5C — the colour already used in the header — puts white text at 7.3:1 and needs no new value in the palette.

Touch targets

48 interactive elements fall below the 44 × 44px minimum at 390px, including the header “Contact Us” at 34px tall, the consent label at 24px, the file-upload trigger at 25px, and every footer link at 24px. The footer links in particular sit in a dense stack where a 24px target with no vertical padding produces frequent mis-taps.

Other

15 of 76 images carry no alt text. Nine CSS rules remove focus outlines on :focus; the page does define :focus-visible styles, so verify each removal is paired with a replacement rather than left bare. The testimonial and badge carousels are the bright spot — both sets of arrows carry proper labels (“Previous review or award”), so someone on this team already knows how to do this.

52 images are served at more than 2.2× their display size — clutch-new.png arrives 324px wide to fill a 40px slot. Not an accessibility issue, but on a page that already runs 9,610px on desktop and 13,185px on mobile, it is free performance.

What works well

  • The hero copy is genuinely well-briefedNaming hedge funds, brokerages, exchanges, FCMs and fintechs in the first sentence, then “low-latency execution and risk controls,” signals domain fluency in a category where most competitors write “innovative fintech solutions.” Do not let a redesign soften this.
  • One CTA, said one wayBook a consultation → Book a Free Consultation → Get in Touch. The page never asks for two things at once, and never invents a fifth verb. That discipline is rarer than it should be.
  • The four-step process block is the strongest thing on the pageIt answers the question every buyer has at a contact form — what happens after I hit send — with commitments rather than adjectives: 24 hours, NDA signed, contract in 1–2 weeks, MVP live in 3–4 months. Consider surfacing an abbreviated version near the hero.
  • A coherent card systemThe same radius, border weight and elevation carry across bento, case study, service and FAQ cards. The page reads as one design, not five.
  • Real product screenshots, not stock illustrationThe bento tiles show actual UI with real numbers. It is the most credible material on the page — which is exactly why the case studies beneath them should carry numbers too.
  • Carousel controls are properly labelled“Previous review or award” is a better aria-label than most production sites ship. The knowledge is in the team; it needs applying to the FAQ and the form.

Priority recommendations

  1. Unblock the conversion path

    Make the consent checkbox focusable, ship the submit button enabled with inline validation, add persistent labels and required markers to the five fields, and give the FAQ rows real button semantics. Nothing else on this page matters if the form cannot be completed and the two commercial FAQ answers cannot be opened.

    Highest impact · roughly one day of front-end work

  2. Fix the green, then the hero ground

    Introduce #0F7A35 as the text-bearing green (5.45:1) and keep #25BB4D for non-text use. Add a left-side scrim to the hero taking the background under the headline to #3A5B5C. Two token changes clear five contrast failures and the page looks essentially unchanged.

    High impact · half a day

  3. Repair the case study block and give it numbers

    Move the client logo out of absolute positioning so it stops landing on the title. Then replace the feature-description summaries with anonymised outcomes — latency, throughput, integrations shipped, time to first live trade. Lead with a trading project rather than the crypto wallet, and promote the investing-fund testimonial to first position.

    High impact · one day build, plus copy from delivery

  4. Set a type scale and cap the measure

    H1 to 60px, H2 to 32px, one 17px body size, max-width: 68ch on all prose. This resolves the flat hierarchy, the 174-character lines, and the three competing body sizes in a single pass, and it will shorten the page.

    Medium impact · half a day

  5. Cut roughly a third of the page

    Merge the four capability lists into two, delete the decorative photo band, trim the badge row to three or four legible marks, and reclaim the space for an engagement model and a price band. At 13,185px on mobile with the form at 84% depth, length is currently costing more than the content is earning.
